# Popular Consensus

## A Trustworthy Place to Think Together — and to Share in What We Create

---

### The Popcorn Jar

Picture a lively county fair, filled with laughter and joyful excitement. At the center of this vibrant scene is a compelling challenge that draws a crowd — a sizable glass bubble filled to the brim with colorful candied popcorn. The task is deceptively simple: estimate the number of kernels, and the entire lot is yours.

As attendees from every walk of life step up and offer their guesses, something extraordinary happens. No individual estimate is particularly close to the truth. The carpenter undershoots. The accountant overshoots. The child guesses a round, optimistic number. The retiree splits the difference. And yet, when you average all of these imperfect, idiosyncratic, sometimes absurd guesses together, the result lands astonishingly close to the actual count.

This is the **Wisdom of the Crowd** — a phenomenon that emerges whenever diverse, independent perspectives are gathered honestly. It is older than the internet, older than democracy, older than any institution we've built to harness it.

But notice something else about the fair. The guesses themselves — the collective intelligence of the crowd — are genuinely valuable. They represent insight that no expert could produce. And in every version of this scene you've ever encountered, the value of that insight has flowed to someone else: the carnival operator, the platform owner, the data broker. The people who actually generated the wisdom walked away with nothing.

Popular Consensus is built on a different premise. The crowd that creates the wisdom should share in what the wisdom is worth.

The world is full of popcorn jars. What do our residents actually want from this budget? Which policy direction has real public support? Are our customers genuinely behind this change, or only the loudest ones? Most of the hardest questions decision-makers face cannot be answered by any single expert or focus group. They can only be answered by listening, honestly and at scale, to the people who have a real stake. The challenge has never been a shortage of opinions — it has been the absence of a trustworthy way to gather them, and a fair way to compensate the people who provide them.

Popular Consensus is built to be both.

### Mission Statement

**Our mission is to give society a trustworthy place to think together — and to ensure that the people who make collective wisdom possible share in the value they create.**

Popular Consensus is a public platform, a public utility, and a cooperative all at once. It allows any group of people, from a neighborhood to a nation, to surface what they collectively believe, want, and value. It is designed from the ground up so that the results can actually be trusted: by the people who participate, by the leaders who consult it, and by the public that lives with the consequences. And it is structured so that the economic value generated by collective participation flows back to the participants themselves rather than being extracted by a platform owner.

We believe the polarization, distrust, and dysfunction visible in today's public conversation are not permanent features of modern life. They are the predictable results of platforms built to maximize attention rather than to surface truth, owned by a handful of companies, and accountable to no one but their shareholders. We also believe the steady erosion of economic dignity — the sense that ordinary participation in public life produces nothing of value for the people doing it — is fueled by the same extractive arrangement. Replace those foundations, and a healthier kind of public conversation becomes possible — one in which people are not just heard, but credited.

We aspire to be more than a product. We aspire to be a piece of civic infrastructure: a common good that bridges divides, gives the underrepresented a real voice, turns the noise of public life into something a leader, a community, or a citizen can actually act on — and recognizes that voice as a legitimate contribution worthy of return.

### What Popular Consensus Is

Popular Consensus is three things at once, built on the same foundation. The first two are familiar functions reimagined. The third is what makes them powerful together.

**1. A social platform owned by its users.** A public-facing space where people post, discuss, ask questions, debate, and vote. Unlike traditional social media, it is not owned by a corporation, governed by a boardroom, or engineered to keep people angry and scrolling. It is owned by the community that uses it, governed by that same community, and designed to surface what people genuinely think rather than what generates the most outrage.

**2. A public utility for measuring consensus.** Anyone — a city council, a school board, a research institute, a non-profit, a company, a national government — can pose a question through Popular Consensus and receive a verifiable, representative answer drawn from real people. Think of it as a shared layer of civic infrastructure: a way for any decision-maker to consult the public honestly, and a way for the public to make itself heard in a form that can be trusted and acted upon.

**3. A Data Union that returns value to the people who create it.** This is where Popular Consensus departs most sharply from anything that has come before. A platform and a utility, taken together, generate something genuinely valuable: a continuous, verifiable record of what real people think, want, and care about. That record has substantial worth to researchers, policymakers, journalists, businesses, and institutions of every kind. On a traditional platform, that value would be harvested by the company and sold off to the highest bidder. Popular Consensus does the opposite. It is structured as a Data Union, so that the value flows back to the people who generated it.

#### What a Data Union Is, and Why It Matters

A Data Union is, in plain terms, a cooperative for data — much like a labor union, but for the digital contributions ordinary people make every day. On their own, individuals have essentially no bargaining power over the data they create; a single person's opinion is easily ignored, and a single person's data is too small to negotiate over. But when thousands or millions of people pool their participation together and negotiate collectively — about who can access the resulting data, on what terms, and for what compensation — they hold something genuinely powerful. The Data Union becomes the bargaining agent. The value flows back to the members. Participants own the polls they create; the people who voted in those polls share in the proceeds when the resulting data is licensed.

This is not a small idea, and it is not a far-off one. The economic logic of modern society is shifting beneath our feet. As automation and artificial intelligence transform the labor market, traditional employment will support fewer people, even as the economic value generated by ordinary human activity — attention, opinion, judgment, civic participation, lived experience — grows enormously. A society in which fewer people work conventional jobs, but more people generate value simply through participation, needs new mechanisms for distributing that value fairly. Without such mechanisms, the gap between those who own data infrastructure and those who merely produce data widens into something genuinely destabilizing.

Data Unions are one of the most promising mechanisms on the table. They are not charity, and they are not a handout. They are a market-based recognition that the people who generate valuable information deserve a share of the proceeds — the same principle that underlies royalties for musicians, residuals for actors, and dividends for shareholders. Popular Consensus extends that principle to civic participation itself.

In a post-labour society — by which we mean a society in which conventional employment can no longer be assumed to support a stable middle class — mechanisms like this become essential. They create dignified, ongoing income streams from activities people would value doing anyway: paying attention to their community, sharing their views, helping their neighbors think through difficult questions. They establish new roles — community organizer, poll author, regional voice — that produce real economic return. And they ensure that the productivity gains of the AI era are not captured entirely by a small number of platforms, but distributed broadly to the people whose participation gives those platforms their value in the first place.

The three pillars are inseparable. The social platform produces engagement. The consensus utility produces insight. The Data Union ensures that the value of both belongs to the people who made them possible. None of the three works as well alone as all three together.

### The Problems We're Addressing

**A distorted picture of public opinion.** Today's platforms reward extremity. They surface the most provocative voices and shelter users inside echo chambers, creating the false impression that the loudest opinions are the most common ones. Leaders making decisions based on what they see online are often responding to a small, intense minority — and assuming it represents the public. It rarely does.

**A collapse of trust in digital information.** A decade of data scandals, hidden moderation rules, and shifting terms of service has left the public deeply skeptical of what they see on the internet. That skepticism is well-founded, but it makes legitimate civic engagement harder for everyone — including the leaders and institutions trying to listen in good faith.

**The absence of a credible instrument for civic listening.** Despite living in the most connected era in human history, we still lack a reliable way to ask large groups of people what they actually think. Traditional polls are expensive, slow, and increasingly inaccurate. Online surveys are easily gamed. Social media metrics measure outrage, not belief. Referenda are rare and costly. We have built no good instrument for the most basic question in democratic life: *What do we, together, actually want?*

**The extraction of value from public participation.** The contemporary internet is built on a quiet bargain: users provide their attention, their opinions, their data, and their labor — and the resulting value is captured almost entirely by the platforms that host them. As that value grows and as traditional employment becomes less reliable, this arrangement is no longer sustainable. People can sense the imbalance even when they cannot articulate it, and that sense corrodes their willingness to participate honestly in public life. A platform that compensates participation, rather than extracting from it, restores the basic fairness that makes good-faith engagement possible again.

### What Makes Popular Consensus Different

Popular Consensus is built around a small set of commitments, each chosen to protect something that traditional platforms routinely fail to protect.

**It is fully transparent.** Every vote, every poll, every governance decision is recorded permanently and publicly. There are no hidden algorithms tuning the results, no back-room moderation rules, no privileged operators with their thumb on the scale. Anyone — journalists, researchers, skeptics — can independently verify what happened.

**It cannot be captured.** No single entity, whether corporate, political, or ideological, can buy the platform, take it over, or tilt its outcomes. There is no headquarters to pressure, no CEO to lobby, no shareholder to please. Ownership is distributed across the people who use it.

**Every voice is a real person.** Participants are verified as unique human beings — one person, one voice. Bot farms, fake accounts, and coordinated manipulation campaigns, which dominate ordinary social media, cannot meaningfully influence results. When a Popular Consensus poll says ten thousand people support something, it means ten thousand actual people. This is also essential to the Data Union: real people generate real value, and the integrity of the participant base is what makes the resulting data worth licensing in the first place.

**Privacy is protected by design.** Users can participate, vote, and speak honestly without exposing themselves to surveillance, retaliation, or having their personal information harvested and sold. You can be counted without being tracked. This is essential for hearing from people who would otherwise stay silent — dissenters, employees, minorities, the vulnerable — and it is essential for a Data Union that traffics in patterns of opinion rather than profiles of individuals.

**The community runs itself.** Content moderation and quality standards are determined by the users themselves, not by a corporate trust-and-safety team in a faraway office. The people who care most about the platform's integrity are the ones who maintain it — and they have a direct stake in keeping it healthy.

**Participants share in what they create.** Users own the polls they author and the data they help generate. When their work proves valuable to researchers, governments, or institutions, they share in that value. The standard social-media bargain — in which users are the product being sold — is inverted. Here, users are the owners, and the platform exists to serve them.

**Governance evolves toward the community.** Popular Consensus launches with a guiding board to provide strategic direction during its early years, then progressively hands over operations to the community itself — becoming, over time, fully self-governing.

### What Success Looks Like

We will know Popular Consensus is working when:

- A city council can credibly consult its residents before a major vote, and the residents trust that the result reflects them.
- A community whose voice has historically been drowned out can demonstrate the real breadth of its support on an issue without having to manufacture virality.
- A journalist can cite a Popular Consensus result the way they once cited Gallup — but with a level of verifiability no traditional pollster can offer.
- A company can hear honestly from its customers, employees, or stakeholders without intermediaries shaping the answer.
- An author of a widely circulated poll receives meaningful compensation when their work informs a government decision, an academic study, or a corporate strategy — the way a musician collects royalties or a journalist is paid for citation.
- A retiree, a student, or a stay-at-home parent can earn a real and ongoing share of income through thoughtful civic participation — not as charity, but as a legitimate return on the value they produce.
- People across political, cultural, and geographic divides discover — often to their own surprise — that they have more in common with one another than the old platforms led them to believe.

### An Invitation

Popular Consensus is an ambitious undertaking. It asks us to rebuild some of the most fundamental infrastructure of public life — the way we talk to one another, the way leaders listen to us, and the way the value of our participation is recognized and returned. It asks us to do this without the centralized control that has historically made such projects easier to build and easier to corrupt. It will require capital, expertise, patience, and the participation of a community willing to believe that the public square can be a public good again, and that the contributions of ordinary people deserve to be honored as such.

We invite you to be part of it. Whether you are an elected official looking for a better way to hear from your constituents, a foundation seeking to strengthen democratic institutions, a policymaker thinking seriously about how value will be distributed in an automating economy, a business that wants honest signal from the people it serves, a researcher studying public opinion, or simply a citizen who has watched the modern internet make public conversation worse — and watched the value of your own participation flow somewhere it shouldn't — there is a place for you here.

The popcorn jar is full. The crowd is gathered. And this time, when we count, the count belongs to everyone who counted.
